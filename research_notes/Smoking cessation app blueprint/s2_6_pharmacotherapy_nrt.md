## Q1. Latest efficacy evidence (Cochrane NMA, NRT and partial-agonist reviews, ORCA trials, FDA status of cytisinicline, apps plus pharmacotherapy)

### Takeaway
The 2023 Cochrane component network meta-analysis (NMA) ranks nicotine e-cigarettes, varenicline and cytisine as the most effective aids, with roughly 2.2–2.4 times the odds of quitting at six months or more (high certainty). Combination NRT comes close behind (OR 1.93) and works better than a single NRT form. Cytisinicline 3 mg three times daily worked in two US/EU phase 3 trials (ORCA-2, ORCA-3), but on 22 June 2026 the FDA issued a **Complete Response Letter rather than an approval**. The letter cited manufacturing (cGMP) problems and unfinished labeling, not efficacy or safety. The company plans to resubmit in Q4 2026 and expects a decision in the first half of 2027. I found **no source** for the claim that "2025–2026 meta-analyses show superior outcomes when apps are paired with NRT/varenicline/cytisine".

### Cited Findings

**Cochrane component NMA: Lindson N, Theodoulou A, Ordóñez-Mena JM, Fanshawe TR, Sutton AJ, Livingstone-Banks J, Hajizadeh A, Zhu S, Aveyard P, Freeman SC, Agrawal S, Hartmann-Boyce J. "Pharmacological and electronic cigarette interventions for smoking cessation in adults: component network meta-analyses." Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2023;9:CD015226. DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD015226.pub2 (PMID 37696529).**
- **Correction to the brief:** the abstract reports **odds ratios (ORs) with 95% credible intervals (CrI)**, not risk ratios (RRs). Outcome: abstinence at six months or more versus no pharmacotherapy, e-cigarette or placebo — [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD015226.pub2/full); [PubMed 37696529](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37696529/)
- Nicotine e-cigarettes: **OR 2.37 (95% CrI 1.73–3.24); 16 RCTs; 3,828 participants; high certainty** — [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD015226.pub2/full)
- Varenicline: **OR 2.33 (95% CrI 2.02–2.68); 67 RCTs; 16,430 participants; high certainty** — [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD015226.pub2/full)
- Cytisine: **OR 2.21 (95% CrI 1.66–2.97); 7 RCTs; 3,848 participants; high certainty** — [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD015226.pub2/full)
- Combination NRT: the authors call it an "additive effect, certainty not rated" and list it among the most effective interventions — [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD015226.pub2/full). A Cochrane overview gives the CNMA estimate for combination NRT as **OR 1.93 (95% CrI 1.61–2.34), certainty not rated**, "closely following" e-cigarettes, varenicline and cytisine — [Livingstone-Banks, Lindson, Hartmann-Boyce 2024, Addiction, DOI 10.1111/add.16624](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.16624)
- Nortriptyline: OR 1.35 (95% CrI 1.02–1.81); 10 RCTs; moderate certainty — [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD015226.pub2/full)
- In absolute terms, the plain-language summary says patch-only NRT and other single-form NRT help **about 8, 9 and 9 per 100 people** quit. The snippet did not show which single form each figure belongs to. I could not see the matching control rate — [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD015226.pub2/full)
- Harms: low-certainty evidence showed **no clear difference in serious adverse events** for nicotine e-cigarettes, varenicline, cytisine or NRT compared with no pharmacotherapy or placebo — [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD015226.pub2/full)
- Search date 29 April 2022. Most studies followed participants for 6–12 months. More than 150,000 people were analysed in total — [University of Oxford news, 12 Sep 2023](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2023-09-12-e-cigarettes-varenicline-and-cytisine-found-be-most-effective-stop-smoking-aids); [Cochrane news](https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/e-cigarettes-varenicline-and-cytisine-are-most-effective-stop-smoking-aids-analysis-over-150000); [drugsandalcohol.ie record](https://www.drugsandalcohol.ie/39552)

**Cochrane overview: Livingstone-Banks J, Lindson N, Hartmann-Boyce J. "Effects of interventions to combat tobacco addiction: Cochrane update of 2021 to 2023 reviews." Addiction 2024;119:2101–2115. DOI 10.1111/add.16624 (PMID 39231467).**
- Covers 5 new reviews, 1 overview and 8 updated reviews — [Addiction 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.16624); [PubMed 39231467](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39231467/)
- There is **high-certainty evidence that combination NRT (a fast-acting form such as gum, lozenge or spray plus a patch) helps more people quit than a single form** — [Addiction 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.16624)
- Evidence on higher NRT doses is mixed. **4 mg gum beat 2 mg only in people with higher dependence** (subgroup analysis) — [Addiction 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.16624)

**Cochrane nicotine receptor partial agonists review: Livingstone-Banks J, Fanshawe TR, Thomas KH, Theodoulou A, Hajizadeh A, Hartman L, Lindson N. - Cytisine vs placebo: **RR 1.30 (95% CI 1.15–1.47); I² = 83%; 4 studies; 4,623 participants; moderate certainty** (downgraded for heterogeneity) — [Cochrane Library CD006103.pub9](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub9/full)
- Varenicline vs placebo: **RR 2.32 (95% CI 2.15–2.51); I² = 60%; 41 studies; 17,395 participants; high certainty** — [Cochrane Library CD006103.pub9](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub9/full)
- Cytisine vs varenicline (head to head): **RR 1.00 (95% CI 0.79–1.26); 2 studies; 2,131 participants; low certainty**, so no clear difference — [Cochrane Library CD006103.pub9](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub9/full). **This conflicts with** an earlier or German-language version (pub8), which reports a pooled RR of **0.83 (95% CI 0.66–1.05)**, moderate certainty, with the point estimate favouring varenicline. The direction of comparison may differ between the two — [Cochrane Library DE, pub8](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/web/cochrane/content?templateType=full&urlTitle=%2Fcdsr%2Fdoi%2F10.1002%2F14651858.CD006103.pub8&doi=10.1002%2F14651858.CD006103.pub8&type=cdsr&contentLanguage=de). - Serious adverse events: varenicline users were more likely to report SAEs than non-users (**RR 1.23, moderate certainty**; the CI was not in the snippet). There was no clear difference between cytisine and placebo — [Cochrane Library CD006103.pub9](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub9/full)

**ORCA-2: Rigotti NA, et al. "Cytisinicline for Smoking Cessation: A Randomized Clinical Trial." JAMA 2023 (July). DOI 10.1001/jama.2023.10042 (DOI as given in the brief; the jamanetwork article ID is 2807079).**

**ORCA-3: Rigotti NA, et al. "Cytisinicline for Smoking Cessation: The ORCA Phase 3 Replication Randomized Clinical Trial." JAMA Intern Med, published online 21 April 2025 (jamanetwork article ID 2832701; PMC12012700).** I did not retrieve the DOI.

### Inferences
- Effect sizes for the blueprint: for "how much a medicine helps vs nothing", use the CNMA ORs (2023). For pairwise placebo-controlled effects, use the partial-agonist review RRs (varenicline RR 2.32, cytisine RR 1.30). Do not mix ORs and RRs in one table. Cytisine's RR vs placebo (1.30) is much lower than its CNMA OR (2.21) because the two analyses use different sets of studies and a different metric. Say so explicitly if both appear.
- ORCA-2 and ORCA-3 are consistent, which supports cytisinicline's efficacy. The CRL was about manufacturing and labeling, so a 2027 US approval is plausible. Still, **the app must not describe cytisinicline as FDA-approved or available in the US as of Oct 2026.**
- 20 June 2026 was a Saturday, which fits an FDA action letter dated Monday 22 June (my calendar reasoning).
- The claim that "2025–2026 meta-analyses show superior outcomes when apps are paired with NRT/varenicline/cytisine" **should not appear in the blueprint as stated**. It is unverified. Defensible wording: "International guidelines (WHO 2024) recommend combining stop-smoking medication with behavioural support, and recommend digital tools as an adjunct; Cochrane evidence shows combining medication with behavioural support improves quit rates" (the second half needs the Hartmann-Boyce citation; see Gaps).

## Q2. NRT dosing per labeling and guidelines, and correct technique (patch, gum, lozenge, other forms, combination NRT, pre-loading, lapses)

### Takeaway
US OTC Drug Facts labels give fixed, published regimens that an app can show word for word, attributed to the label. Patch: more than 10 cigarettes per day (CPD) starts at 21 mg for 6 weeks, then 14 mg for 2 weeks, then 7 mg for 2 weeks. Gum and lozenge: 4 mg if the first cigarette is within 30 minutes of waking; 12-week taper; at most 24 gum pieces or 20 lozenges a day. Gum uses a chew-slowly-until-tingle, then park technique for about 30 minutes, with no eating or drinking 15 minutes before or during. Cochrane overview evidence supports combination NRT and pre-loading. Detail from UK (NICE NG209/NCSCT) and the US guidelines (Fiore 2008, ATS 2020) could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings

**Nicotine gum (Nicorette, nicotine polacrilex, US Drug Facts):**
- Strength: **4 mg if the first cigarette is within 30 minutes of waking; 2 mg if after 30 minutes** — [DailyMed, Nicorette gum](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=673fd19f-69b3-4f73-b9b4-18b5f4cbf69c); [Nicorette dosing page](https://www.nicorette.com/usage-science/using-nicorette/nicotine-gum-dosage)
- 12-week schedule: **weeks 1–6, 1 piece every 1–2 h; weeks 7–9, 1 piece every 2–4 h; weeks 10–12, 1 piece every 4–8 h** — [DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=673fd19f-69b3-4f73-b9b4-18b5f4cbf69c)
- **No more than 24 pieces a day. Use at least 9 pieces a day for the first 6 weeks** to improve the chance of quitting — [Nicorette dosing page](https://www.nicorette.com/usage-science/using-nicorette/nicotine-gum-dosage); [Nicorette 4 mg product page](https://www.nicorette.com/4mg-nicotine-gum-mint-170-ct/). The 2 mg and 4 mg product pages word these limits slightly differently, so take the wording from the specific package insert.
- Technique: **chew slowly until it tingles, then park it between cheek and gum. Repeat until most of the tingle is gone (about 30 minutes). Do not eat or drink for 15 minutes before chewing, or while chewing a piece** — [DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=673fd19f-69b3-4f73-b9b4-18b5f4cbf69c); [Nicorette dosing page](https://www.nicorette.com/usage-science/using-nicorette/nicotine-gum-dosage); [UW-CTRI nicotine gum guide](https://ctri.wisc.edu/smokers/medications/nicotine-gum/)
- For strong cravings, a second piece may be used within the hour, but **do not chew one piece right after another** — [Nicorette dosing page](https://www.nicorette.com/usage-science/using-nicorette/nicotine-gum-dosage)

**Nicotine patch (NicoDerm CQ, US Drug Facts):**
- **More than 10 CPD: Step 1, 21 mg/day for weeks 1–6; Step 2, 14 mg/day for weeks 7–8; Step 3, 7 mg/day for weeks 9–10** — [DailyMed NicoDerm CQ label PDF](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/getFile.cfm?setid=93b2d1b9-83c1-40b5-b6af-90c38c8d6cef&type=pdf); [OTCLabels, NicoDerm CQ](https://otclabels.com/lib/otc/otc-meds/nicoderm-cq/)
- **10 CPD or fewer: do not use Step 1 (21 mg); start at Step 2 (14 mg)** — [OTCLabels](https://otclabels.com/lib/otc/otc-meds/nicoderm-cq/); [DailyMed PDF](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/getFile.cfm?setid=93b2d1b9-83c1-40b5-b6af-90c38c8d6cef&type=pdf)
- **Start on the quit day.** Apply to clean, dry, hairless skin. Use **one new patch every 24 hours** — [OTCLabels](https://otclabels.com/lib/otc/otc-meds/nicoderm-cq/); [FDA NDA 020165 labeling document](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2002/020165_S020_NICODERM%20CQ.pdf)
- **Used patches still contain enough nicotine to be dangerous. Keep them away from children and pets** — [OTCLabels](https://otclabels.com/lib/otc/otc-meds/nicoderm-cq/)

**Nicotine lozenge (US Drug Facts):**
- **4 mg if the first cigarette is within 30 minutes of waking; 2 mg otherwise** — [UCSF Rx for Change lozenge handout](https://rxforchange.ucsf.edu/download.php?file=Nicotine+lozenge.pdf); [DailyMed lozenge label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=991704ed-781a-489b-8b56-0b558e8fc385)

**Combination NRT and pre-loading (evidence):**
- Patch plus a fast-acting form beats a single form (high certainty). Pre-loading before the quit date is supported. 4 mg beats 2 mg gum only in people with higher dependence — [Livingstone-Banks et al. 2024, Addiction, DOI 10.1111/add.16624](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.16624)
- WHO 2024 lists **combination NRT (patch plus a short-acting form such as gum or lozenge)** as an option for people quitting with NRT — [WHO guideline executive summary](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/n/who377825/fm-ch2/); [WHO news release](https://www.who.int/news/item/02-07-2024-who-releases-first-ever-clinical-treatment-guideline-for-tobacco-cessation-in-adults)

### Inferences
- An app can safely show **the label's own schedule table**, citing the product's Drug Facts, after the user selects their product and strength. The choices themselves (4 mg vs 2 mg from time to first cigarette; patch step from CPD) are **printed label rules**. Showing them as "what your label says" is different from the app calculating a dose. See Q5 for why the app should not auto-select or titrate.
- The label instruction is "do not eat or drink for 15 minutes before or while chewing". The common coaching line "avoid acidic drinks such as coffee, soda and juice" is a clinical elaboration, **not label text** (source not retrieved; see Gaps). Present it as coaching advice, not as a label quote.
- The "at least 9 pieces/lozenges a day in weeks 1–6" minimum is the label's own anti-underdosing instruction. It is a natural adherence target the app can display and track against, entered by the user and labeled as the label's recommendation.
- The patch schedule (10 weeks) and the gum/lozenge schedule (12 weeks) differ in length. The app's program timeline has to follow the product the user enters.

## Q3. Common misuse and underdosing, and how adherence relates to abstinence

### Takeaway
The labels themselves guard against underdosing: at least 9 pieces or lozenges a day in weeks 1–6, a full 10–12-week course, and a stronger product for heavier dependence. The evidence that higher dependence benefits from higher strength (4 mg gum) is in the Cochrane overview. I could not retrieve this session the primary studies quantifying NRT/varenicline adherence and its link to abstinence (e.g., Shiffman 2008; Raupach 2014), or the Cochrane adherence-intervention review.

### Cited Findings
- The labels set a **minimum use (at least 9 gum pieces or lozenges a day for the first 6 weeks)** "to improve your chances of quitting". Underdosing is the problem the label itself is guarding against — [DailyMed lozenge label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=991704ed-781a-489b-8b56-0b558e8fc385); [Nicorette dosing page](https://www.nicorette.com/usage-science/using-nicorette/nicotine-gum-dosage)
- Gum label warns against chewing one piece right after another and specifies slow chewing and parking — [Nicorette dosing page](https://www.nicorette.com/usage-science/using-nicorette/nicotine-gum-dosage); [DailyMed gum](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=673fd19f-69b3-4f73-b9b4-18b5f4cbf69c)
- The benefit of 4 mg over 2 mg gum appears limited to people with higher dependence. So matching strength to dependence (the label's 30-minute rule) matters — [Livingstone-Banks et al. 2024, Addiction](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.16624)
- In ORCA-3, the 12-week cytisinicline course gave higher end-of-treatment abstinence (30.3% vs 9.4%) than the 6-week course (14.8% vs 6.0%), measured over different windows. The trial is evidence for completing the full course length — [PMC12012700](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12012700/)

### Inferences
- The app's adherence metrics should mirror the label: daily count against the label minimum (9/day, weeks 1–6) and maximum (24 gum or 20 lozenges per day; 5 lozenges per 6 h), patch-on days, and progress through the course. Show deviations as prompts to **check the label or ask a pharmacist**. The app should never tell the user to take more.
- Coaching on gum technique is a reasonable app feature. The label technique (chew slowly, park, about 30 min) can be shown in animation. Side-effect logging (hiccups, nausea, jaw pain, mouth irritation) can prompt a technique review or a pharmacist conversation. The link between fast chewing and these side effects is prior knowledge and not sourced this session; see Gaps.
- The ORCA duration findings plus the label course lengths support "finish the course" nudges. Whether the app can affect actual medication adherence is unproven (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- **Shiffman S et al. 2008** (NRT compliance and its relation to outcomes; I am unsure whether it is the Nicotine Tob Res or Addiction paper), **Raupach T et al. 2014** (varenicline adherence and abstinence; Eur Respir J?) and the Cochrane review **"Interventions for increasing adherence to medications for tobacco dependence"** (Hollands GJ et al. 2019, CD009164.pub3) were **not retrieved**. ## Q5. Regulatory guardrails for a wellness app, and design of a safe NRT/medication module

### Takeaway
The FDA explicitly lists **software that tracks medications and provides user-configured reminders** among functions where it **intends to exercise enforcement discretion**. Its guidance also lists coaching patients to follow a **pre-determined** dosing schedule by simple prompting. Apple's App Review Guideline **1.4.2 says drug dosage calculators must come from approved entities** (manufacturer, hospital, university, insurer, pharmacy) or have FDA or equivalent approval. A consumer wellness app should therefore stick to **user-entered regimens, reminders, label-sourced education, technique coaching, logging and escalation**, and **never calculate, select or titrate doses**.

### Cited Findings
- FDA's examples page lists among the enforcement-discretion examples: **"Software functions that keep track of medications and provide user-configured reminders for improved medication adherence."** The page states that such functions "may meet the definition of medical device, but the FDA intends to exercise enforcement discretion … because they pose lower risk to the public" — [FDA, Examples of Software Functions for Which the FDA Will Exercise Enforcement Discretion](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-software-functions-including-mobile-medical-applications/examples-software-functions-which-fda-will-exercise-enforcement-discretion)
- The guidance ("Policy for Device Software Functions and Mobile Medical Applications", section V.B) covers software that helps patients self-manage a condition **without providing specific treatment suggestions**. One example is **coaching patients to adhere to pre-determined medication dosing schedules by simple prompting** — [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/policy-device-software-functions-and-mobile-medical-applications); [Innolitics guidance summary](https://innolitics.com/articles/fda-guidance-policy-for-device-software-functions-and-mobile-medical-applications/); [Morgan Lewis slides](https://www.morganlewis.com/-/media/files/publication/presentation/webinar/mobilemedicalappswebinar_09oct13.pdf)
- Enforcement discretion is **not an exemption**. The FDA can change its position if a product turns out to pose an unexpected risk — [Innolitics](https://innolitics.com/articles/fda-guidance-policy-for-device-software-functions-and-mobile-medical-applications/); [NIH SEED: FDA's enforcement discretion policy](https://seed.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/FDAs-Enforcement-Discretion-Policy.pdf)
- **Apple Guideline 1.4.2 (Safety, Physical Harm):** drug dosage calculators must come from the drug manufacturer, a hospital, university, health insurance company, pharmacy or other approved entity, or receive approval from the FDA or one of its international counterparts — [Apple Developer Forums thread 774177](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/774177); [ConductAtlas provision record](https://conductatlas.com/platform/apple/apple-app-store-review-guidelines/provision/CA-P-018798/drug-dosage-calculators-must-come-from-approved-entities/); [MobiHealthNews 2017](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/apple-gets-tough-medication-dosage-apps)
- **Apple 1.4.1:** medical apps that could provide inaccurate data or be used to diagnose or treat patients get greater scrutiny. Apps with regulatory clearance should link to the documentation — [MobiHealthNews](https://mobihealthnews.com/node/138971); [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/apple-raises-entry-bar-for-medical-health-apps/425810/)
- Apple has not publicly defined "drug dosage calculator". A developer asked whether reference-only displays or simple arithmetic on user-entered doses count and got no official answer — [Apple Developer Forums thread 774177](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/774177)

### Inferences (design proposal for a safe NRT and medication module; derived from the sources above, not legal advice)
1. **The regimen is user-entered and label-anchored.** The user picks the product type (patch, gum, lozenge, other) and **enters the strength and schedule from their pack's Drug Facts or SmPC, or from their clinician or pharmacist**. The app may show the published label schedule for the selected product **as a quoted, attributed reference** ("Your pack's Drug Facts says…"). It should not compute a personalized dose. Asking "time to first cigarette" and auto-assigning 4 mg vs 2 mg, or "CPD" and auto-assigning a patch step, edges toward a dosage calculator (Apple 1.4.2) and toward treatment-specific suggestions (outside FDA's enforcement-discretion examples). The safer pattern is to show the label rule as text and let the user choose.
2. **Reminders only.** Users configure them (times, intervals, taper dates copied from their regimen). This matches the FDA example of "user-configured reminders for improved medication adherence".
3. **No automatic titration or dose change.** No "increase to X" or "you can stop now" logic. Taper steps happen only because the user entered those dates. If logged use is below the label's minimum or above its maximum, show "check your label / talk to a pharmacist", never "take more" or "take less".
4. **Technique coaching.** Use an animation or video of the label's chew-slowly, tingle, park, repeat (~30 min) technique, plus the no-eating-or-drinking 15-minute rule, quoted from the label. Patch-site and disposal safety come from the label (used patches dangerous to children and pets).
5. **Side-effect logging with escalation.** Logging hiccups, nausea, jaw or mouth soreness, skin irritation, sleep disturbance or palpitations should trigger "review technique" content plus a "talk to a pharmacist or doctor" prompt. **Red flags trigger immediate escalation copy:** chest pain, irregular heartbeat, signs of nicotine overdose, pregnancy or breastfeeding, recent heart attack or stroke, and mood changes or suicidal thoughts on varenicline. The exact wording must be taken from the relevant label warnings (not retrieved; see Gaps).
6. **Disclaimers.** "Follow your product label and your clinician's or pharmacist's instructions. This app does not provide medical advice or determine doses." Avoid claims of diagnosis or treatment. If the app ever adds dose logic, it needs regulatory clearance and Apple-approved-entity status.
7. **Prescription medicines (varenicline, cytisine).** The user enters the regimen exactly as prescribed or as on the leaflet. The app provides reminders and adherence logging, and educates using label-quoted content only, without schedule suggestions. Country-aware: hide or flag products not licensed in the user's market. Cytisinicline is **not approved in the US** as of Oct 2026.

### Gaps
- I did not see the **current version date** of the FDA "Policy for Device Software Functions and Mobile Medical Applications" guidance; sources disagreed. Prior knowledge (UNVERIFIED): final version 28 Sept 2022, issued alongside the final **Clinical Decision Support Software** guidance, which matters if the app gives patient-facing recommendations (patient-facing CDS falls outside the non-device CDS criteria). Also check whether the FDA updated the General Wellness or CDS guidance in 2025–2026.
- I did not see the **full current text of Apple Guidelines 1.4.1 and 1.4.2**, including whether 1.4.1 still says apps "should remind users to check with a doctor… before making medical decisions" (prior knowledge, unverified). Check https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/.
- **EU MDR** (Regulation (EU) 2017/745) **Rule 11** and **MDCG 2019-11** qualification and classification of software were **not retrieved**. Prior knowledge: software that provides information used to make therapeutic decisions is generally class IIa or higher, while pure reminders or logging may fall outside the definition of a medical device. Not verified.
- **Google Play** health app policies, **UK MHRA** software/AI as a medical device guidance, and **India CDSCO** rules for software as a medical device were not researched.
- Exact label "Ask a doctor before use / Stop use" wording for gum, lozenge and patch, and the varenicline Medication Guide warnings, were not retrieved. They are needed for red-flag copy.

## Q6. Regional differences: WHO 2024 guideline, WHO Essential Medicines List, OTC availability

### Takeaway
WHO's first clinical treatment guideline for tobacco cessation (2 July 2024) recommends **varenicline, NRT (including combination NRT), bupropion and cytisine**. It also recommends behavioural support (brief advice of 30 s–3 min plus intensive support) and **digital interventions as an adjunct or self-management tool**. The **2025 WHO Model List of Essential Medicines (24th list, released 5 Sept 2025) added cytisine to the core list**, alongside NRT, varenicline and bupropion. OTC status by country (for example India) could not be verified this session.

### Cited Findings
- WHO released its first-ever clinical treatment guideline for tobacco cessation in adults on **2 July 2024** — [WHO news release](https://www.who.int/news/item/02-07-2024-who-releases-first-ever-clinical-treatment-guideline-for-tobacco-cessation-in-adults)
- Pharmacological recommendations: **varenicline, NRT, bupropion and cytisine**. **Combination NRT** (patch plus gum or lozenge) is an option for NRT users. Varenicline and combination NRT are described as more effective than NRT monotherapy and bupropion — [WHO executive summary, NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/n/who377825/fm-ch2/); [Medscape](https://reference.medscape.com/cc2/p10/who-clinical-guideline-tobacco-cessation-adults-2024a1000kj1); [WHO guideline PDF via ADHA](https://www.adha.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/WHO_Tobacco_Cessation_Clinical_Treatment_Guideline_2024.pdf)
