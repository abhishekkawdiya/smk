## Q1. FDA "General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices": the January 2026 revision and the two claim categories

### Takeaway
FDA did issue a revised final General Wellness guidance on **January 6, 2026**, on the same day as a revised Clinical Decision Support (CDS) software guidance. It replaced the 2019 version without a draft or comment period. The two-part test (intended only for general wellness, and low risk) and the two claim categories are unchanged. What is new is explicit room for **non-invasive wearables that estimate physiologic parameters** (for example blood pressure, SpO2, glucose, HRV), on condition that the outputs are framed for wellness only. That means no disease names, no diagnostic thresholds, no "clinical-grade" or "medical-grade" accuracy claims, and no alerts meant to manage a condition.

- **Category 1 (general state of health, no disease reference).** Claims about sustaining or improving general health or healthy activity without naming a disease. Examples include weight management, physical fitness, sleep management, self-esteem, relaxation or stress management, mental acuity, and sexual function. — [Fish & Richardson (2015 draft analysis)](https://www.fr.com/insights/thought-leadership/articles/fdas-draft-wellness-guidance-potentially-expands-the-universe-of-unregulated-products/); [Thryve 2026 summary](https://www.thryve.health/blog/general-wellness-guidance-2026) [via search snippet]

- **Background summary.** The Congressional Research Service published an Insight on FDA oversight of general wellness products — [CRS IN12657](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12657) [blocked; not fetched]

### Inferences
- **Wearable data.** The app imports RHR/HRV from HealthKit or Health Connect and does not sense anything itself. The 2026 revision is helpful here: displaying those trends with wellness framing is squarely within the expanded policy. The app must avoid AFib, arrhythmia, "cardiac damage" or clinical-threshold language.
- **Lung Age and the Bio-Toxicity Index.** These are modeled estimates from user inputs, not sensor measurements. The 2026 emphasis on validation and on not mimicking clinical measurements cuts both ways. Showing a single number that looks like a clinical value (an age in years, a "toxicity" score) invites scrutiny. FDA's validation expectation for physiologic estimates suggests the app should present these as illustrative, population-based estimates and avoid any accuracy claims it cannot validate.
- **Category 2 claims.** A claim like "Being smoke-free, as part of a healthy lifestyle, may help reduce the risk of heart disease" is structurally a Category 2 claim, since the smoking/CVD link is well accepted. Whether FDA would treat smoking cessation itself as a "healthy lifestyle choice" is not settled by any source I found (see Q2). A conservative build keeps marketing in Category 1 and uses Category 2 phrasing sparingly, with counsel sign-off.
- **Escalation prompts.** A one-time prompt such as "If you notice symptoms that concern you, consider talking to a healthcare professional" fits the town-hall guardrails. A recurring alert like "Your recovery is abnormal, see a doctor" does not.

### Gaps
- I could not read FDA's 2026 guidance text, so the exact wording of the new non-invasive-sensing section, the illustrative examples and any "values" or validation language is unconfirmed beyond law-firm summaries.
- Whether the 2026 revision added or removed examples about respiration, breathing or software-only apps (as opposed to wearables) is not established.
- The WHOOP close-out (June 2026) comes from a single secondary source.

## Q2. How FDA views smoking-cessation claims, and the Device Software Functions / Mobile Medical Apps policy

### Takeaway
FDA regulates quit-smoking medicines (NRT, varenicline, bupropion) as drugs. Sensor-based quit aids have gone through device clearance (for example, the Pivot CO breath sensor 510(k)). For software, FDA lists "periodic educational information, reminders, or motivational guidance" for smokers trying to quit as an **enforcement-discretion** example. That listing means FDA regards such software as potentially meeting the device definition but does not intend to enforce requirements against it. It is not a statement that the software is a non-device. Claims of treating nicotine dependence, or quoting quit-rate efficacy, move a product toward drug/device or digital-therapeutic territory. Precedents include the Pivot clearance, CureApp SC (Japan) and the German DiGA NichtraucherHelden (EU).

### Cited Findings
- **Enforcement-discretion example on smoking.** FDA's list includes software that provides "periodic educational information, reminders, or motivational guidance" to pregnant people, smokers trying to quit, or people recovering from addiction. — [FDA examples page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-software-functions-including-mobile-medical-applications/examples-software-functions-which-fda-will-exercise-enforcement-discretion) [via search snippet; page blocked]
- **Enforcement-discretion example on coping skills.** The same list covers software that helps patients with diagnosed psychiatric conditions (PTSD, depression, anxiety, OCD) maintain behavioral coping skills, for example a "Skill of the Day" or audio messages during heightened anxiety. — [FDA examples page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-software-functions-including-mobile-medical-applications/examples-software-functions-which-fda-will-exercise-enforcement-discretion); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/21-types-health-apps-fda-could-regulate-wont) [via search snippet]
- **Medication reminders.** Secondary sources say FDA routinely applies enforcement discretion to self-management software, including medication reminders. — [Bipartisan Policy Center](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/issue-brief/fda-oversight-understanding-the-regulation-of-health-ai-tools/); [Nixon Law Group](https://www.nixonlawgroup.com/resources/fda-enforcement-discretion-a-strategic-path-to-market-for-digital-health-and-digital-therapeutics-companies) [via search snippet; FDA's own medication-reminder wording UNVERIFIED]
- **What enforcement discretion means.** FDA keeps its authority under the FD&C Act but chooses not to enforce for these low-risk functions. Its list is non-exhaustive. — [FDA examples page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-software-functions-including-mobile-medical-applications/examples-software-functions-which-fda-will-exercise-enforcement-discretion); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/21-types-health-apps-fda-could-regulate-wont) [via search snippet]
- **Governing guidance.** "Policy for Device Software Functions and Mobile Medical Applications" — [FDA PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/80958/download) [blocked]. The September 2022 version date is taken from the brief and was not re-verified. I found no report that the January 2026 actions revised this guidance; reports mention only the General Wellness and CDS guidances.
- **Drugs for quitting.** FDA's consumer page "Smoking: Medicines To Help You Quit" covers FDA-approved cessation medicines — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/womens-health-topics/smoking-medicines-help-you-quit) [title via search; content not fetched]
- **Device precedent (US).** The Pivot carbon-monoxide breath sensor (Carrot) received an expanded 510(k) indication in 2021 for over-the-counter use as part of a smoking-cessation program, based on a single-arm study. — [MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2021/07/carrot-gets-fda-nod-for-over-the-counter-use-of-smoking-cessation-device/) [via search snippet]
- **Device precedent (Japan).** CureApp's quit-smoking app was approved as Japan's first digital therapeutic. — [ST Office Tokyo](https://stofficetokyo.ch/news/startups/quit-smoking-app-greenlighted-as-japans-first-digital-therapeutic) [via search snippet]
- **Device precedent (EU/Germany).** NichtraucherHelden is permanently listed as a DiGA for ICD-10 F17.2 (tobacco dependence), as a class I medical device for treating diagnosed tobacco dependence in adults, with a minimum age of 18. — [psychenet fact sheet](https://psychenet.de/images/Online_Interventionen/Steckbrief_NichtraucherHelden_JA_MK_berarbeitet.pdf); [Pharmazeutische Zeitung](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/mit-dem-rauchen-aufhoeren-mit-digitaler-unterstuetzung-137255/); [IWW](https://www.iww.de/aaa/verordnung/gesundheits-apps-zwei-apps-gegen-tabakabhaengigkeit-im-diga-verzeichnis-f155519) [via search snippet]
- **NRT drug-development friction.** An Epstein Becker Green attorney noted at an FDA NRT hearing that coaching apps complicate NRT trials. They raise the bar a drug must beat against placebo and create uncertainty about whether an app must be referenced in a submission. — [EBG](https://www.ebglaw.com/news/james-boiani-quoted-in-nicotine-replacement-trials-and-the-app-effect/) [via search snippet]

### Inferences
- **"Helps you quit smoking."** FDA puts motivational and reminder apps for smokers in the enforcement-discretion bucket rather than the "not a device" bucket. So a plain quit-support claim is best treated as a low-risk device-type claim that FDA currently tolerates, not a pure wellness claim. Claims of **treating nicotine addiction or withdrawal**, **specific quit-rate efficacy** ("doubles your chance of quitting"), or **replacing NRT or medication** go beyond the reminder/motivation examples and invite device or drug scrutiny. They also need RCT-level substantiation under FTC guidance (Q3).
- **Precedents show the line.** A CO **measurement** used for quitting was cleared as a device (Pivot). The app's Bio-Toxicity Index is a **modeled** CO/recovery estimate. It should never be shown as measured ppm or COHb and never compared with breath-CO devices.
- **NRT reminders.** User-configured reminders plus label-consistent technique tips fit FDA's reminder examples. Recommending a dose or strength, or titrating it, would resemble a dosing function. That raises FDA risk and also triggers Apple 1.4.2 (Q7).

### Gaps
- I found no FDA document that explicitly calls "helps you quit smoking" a device claim for a nicotine-free app, or that places smoking cessation in General Wellness Category 2.
- I could not confirm FDA's exact enforcement-discretion wording for medication reminders, or whether the 2022 DSF/MMA guidance has been revised since.
- The Pivot 510(k) number and its exact indication wording were not checked in FDA's database (blocked).

## Q3. FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (2024), FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance, and Washington's My Health My Data Act

### Takeaway
A direct-to-consumer health app outside HIPAA is squarely covered by the amended **FTC HBNR** (effective **July 29, 2024**). Under the amended rule, an unauthorized *disclosure*, such as sharing data with ad SDKs, counts as a breach. The **FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance** (Dec 20, 2022) sets "competent and reliable scientific evidence" as the bar for health claims, usually meaning RCTs, and says hedges like "may help" do not cure an unsupported claim. **Washington's MHMDA** (in force March 31, 2024; June 30, 2024 for small businesses) requires opt-in consent for consumer health data and carries a private right of action.

### Cited Findings
- **HBNR timeline.** The final rule was adopted April 26, 2024, published in the Federal Register May 30, 2024, and took effect July 29, 2024. — [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/08/ftc-updated-health-breach-notification-rule); [Quarles](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/ftc-publishes-final-rule-amending-health-breach-notification-rule); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2024/07/final-changes-to-health-breach-notification) [via search snippet]
- **HBNR scope.** The amendments clarify that the rule covers direct-to-consumer health apps and similar technologies not regulated by HIPAA. "PHR identifiable health information" now includes health information arising from use of apps or online services, including information inferred from non-health data. — [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/08/ftc-updated-health-breach-notification-rule); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/pdf/resources/insights/240514-ftc-issues-final-rule-to-expand) [via search snippet]
- **Persistent identifiers.** Device and advertising IDs count as PHR identifiable health information when combined with health information and able to identify or re-identify a person. — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2024/07/final-changes-to-health-breach-notification) [via search snippet]
- **"Breach" includes unauthorized disclosure.** The definition covers unauthorized disclosures, not only intrusions, which codifies the FTC's 2021 policy statement and later enforcement. — [Quarles](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/ftc-publishes-final-rule-amending-health-breach-notification-rule); [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/08/ftc-updated-health-breach-notification-rule) [via search snippet]
- **HBNR penalties.** Civil penalties are reported at up to $51,744 per violation, adjusted annually for inflation. — [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/08/ftc-updated-health-breach-notification-rule) [via search snippet; current inflation-adjusted figure UNVERIFIED]
- **Health Products Compliance Guidance: scope and date.** Issued Dec 20, 2022, replacing the 1998 dietary-supplement guide. It applies to health-related claims generally. — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2023/01/ftc-issues-new-guidance-on-health-related-claims-to-replace-the-dietary-supplements-advertising-guide); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/pt/insights/2023/01/ftc-overhauls-and-expands-25yearold-health-products-advertising-compliance-guide) [via search snippet]
- **Substantiation standard.** Health claims need "competent and reliable scientific evidence." Adequate substantiation will generally need to be "randomized, controlled human clinical testing." Quality matters more than quantity. — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/ftc-issues-significant-updated-guidance-for-health-related-claims/); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/pt/insights/2023/01/ftc-overhauls-and-expands-25yearold-health-products-advertising-compliance-guide) [via search snippet]
- **Weak evidence and hedges.** Animal or in-vitro studies, anecdotes and general public-health recommendations are usually insufficient on their own. Qualifiers like "may," "helps," "promising" and "preliminary" are likely inadequate to cure an unsupported claim. "Results not typical" disclaimers do not cure deceptive testimonials. — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/icymi-a-new-year-and-new-guidance-from-ftc-on); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/ftc-issues-significant-updated-guidance-for-health-related-claims/) [via search snippet]
- **MHMDA effective dates and scope.** In force March 31, 2024 (June 30, 2024 for small businesses). It targets consumer health data held by entities outside HIPAA, which makes it broad in practice. — [Stoel Rives FAQ](https://www.stoel.com/insights/publications/faq-washington-states-my-health-my-data-act); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/my-health-my-data-act-targets-entities-not-subject-to-hipaa-and-provides-a-private-right-of-action) [via search snippet]
- **MHMDA consent.** Collection requires affirmative consent for a specified purpose unless the data is necessary to provide the requested service. Consumers can access and delete data and withdraw consent. — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2023/04/my-health-my-data-washington-enacts-first-state-comprehensive-health-privacy-law) [via search snippet]
- **MHMDA enforcement.** Enforced by the Washington AG and through a private right of action under the Washington Consumer Protection Act. The first private suit was filed Feb 10, 2025 against Amazon. — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/my-health-my-data-act-targets-entities-not-subject-to-hipaa-and-provides-a-private-right-of-action); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/de-DE/Insights/2025/02/First-Lawsuit-Filed-Under-Washingtons-My-Health-My-Data-Act) [via search snippet]

### Inferences
- **HBNR status.** The app combines wearable data (HealthKit/Health Connect) with user logs. It is therefore very likely a "vendor of personal health records" or a PHR-related entity under the HBNR. Any analytics or ads SDK that receives health-linked events without authorization could amount to a reportable breach. Recommended defaults: no third-party ad SDKs, analytics events stripped of health payloads, and first-party analytics with explicit opt-in.
- **FTC substantiation.** The Lung Age, Bio-Toxicity and "recovery" visualizations are health claims. Each needs (a) published scientific grounding for the model and (b) honest framing as an estimate. Quit-rate or "craving prevention" efficacy claims would need app-specific RCT evidence. Without it, describe features rather than outcomes.
- **MHMDA consent design.** Build a separate consent for collecting health data (including microphone-derived breath data and wearable HRV/RHR) and a further separate consent for any sharing. Nothing beyond what is needed to deliver the feature should be on by default.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the HBNR's notification deadlines and FTC-notice thresholds against the rule text, or the current inflation-adjusted penalty.
- MHMDA details not confirmed by these sources: the geofencing ban, signed authorization for "sale", and a separate consumer-health-data privacy policy. Counsel should confirm against RCW 19.373.
- I did not confirm whether the FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance names apps or software explicitly.
- Other states' consumer-health-data laws (e.g., Nevada, Connecticut amendments) were not researched.

## Q4. EU: MDR Rule 11, MDCG 2019-11 (Rev.1, 2025), Article 2(1) "prediction, prognosis", the AI Act, and GDPR Article 9

### Takeaway
Under the MDR, software is a medical device only if the manufacturer's intended purpose is a medical one under Art. 2(1). That list explicitly includes "prediction" and "prognosis" of disease. Software for lifestyle and well-being is excluded. If the app *were* qualified as a device, Rule 11 would normally put decision-informing software at **class IIa or higher**, and only "all other" software at class I. So staying outside qualification depends entirely on claims and design. MDCG 2019-11 **Rev.1 (June 17, 2025)** clarified the guidance without changing its logic. The AI Act's high-risk regime for AI in MDR devices now applies from **Aug 2, 2028**, after the Digital Omnibus (reported as Reg. (EU) 2026/1744). GDPR Art. 9 makes wearable and breath-derived health data special-category data, which requires **explicit consent** and, almost certainly, a **DPIA**.

### Cited Findings
- **Rule 11, decision-support software.** Software that provides information used to take diagnostic or therapeutic decisions is class IIa. It becomes IIb if a decision could cause serious deterioration of health or surgical intervention, and class III if it could cause death or irreversible deterioration. — [NAMSA](https://namsa.com/resources/blog/mdr-stricter-rules-medical-device-software/); [Penningtons Manches](https://www.penningtonslaw.com/insights/new-guidance-on-the-classification-of-software-as-medical-devices/) [via search snippet]
- **Rule 11, monitoring software.** Software that monitors physiological processes is IIa. It is IIb when it monitors vital parameters whose variations could result in immediate danger to the patient. All other software is class I. Sources differ on whether the text says "immediate danger" or "immediate damage"; check the OJ text. — [NAMSA](https://namsa.com/resources/blog/mdr-stricter-rules-medical-device-software/); [Penningtons Manches](https://www.penningtonslaw.com/insights/new-guidance-on-the-classification-of-software-as-medical-devices/) [via search snippet]
- **Rule 11 in practice.** Bristows notes how broad Rule 11 is (it would even capture thermometer-like software). MDCG 2021-24 says human review does not automatically lower the class. — [Bristows](https://www.bristows.com/news/deciphering-rule-11-new-guidance-on-the-classification-of-software-medical-devices-under-the-eu-mdr); [OpenRegulatory on MDCG 2021-24](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2021-24) [via search snippet]
- **Art. 2(1) purposes.** The MDR lists "diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease." Johner Institute criticizes the MDCG for folding prognosis into diagnosis. — [Johner Institute](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/mdr-rule-11/) [via search snippet]; statutory text at [EUR-Lex 2017/745](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj) [blocked; not fetched]
- **Well-being exclusion.** The MDR text says software intended for lifestyle and well-being purposes is not a medical device, and the 2025 revision reportedly confirms that fitness and wellness apps are not devices. - **GDPR, explicit consent.** Fitness and wearable data is health data under Art. 9 where it tells the user something about their health. Explicit consent needs an express statement, such as a separate affirmative confirmation, and cannot be inferred from accepting general terms. — [Thryve](https://www.thryve.health/blog/wearable-data-special-category-gdpr); [GDPR Local](https://gdprlocal.com/gdpr-for-wearable-technology/); [Momentum](https://www.themomentum.ai/blog/gdpr-consent-requirements-health-data) [via search snippet; commercial blogs]; statutory text at [EUR-Lex 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) [not fetched]

### Inferences
- **Lung Age.** An individualized "your lungs are X years old" output is easily read as **prediction or prognosis** about a body organ, and "accelerated lung aging" is a COPD-risk concept. If marketing links it to lung disease risk ("predicts COPD"), the app falls into Art. 2(1) and then Rule 11 class IIa or higher. Mitigations: present it as an educational illustration of published population averages, never as an individual health assessment; no disease names; no "normal/abnormal" bands; and a clear statement that it is not a lung-function test.
- **Craving-risk prediction.** This predicts a *behavioral* event, not a disease. But nicotine dependence is a coded disorder (F17), so "relapse prediction" for "nicotine addiction" can be framed as prognosis or monitoring of a disease. Use habit and pattern language.
- **Acoustic breath and cough tracking.** Breath *pacing* for relaxation is a wellness function. "Cough tracking" tied to lung-health deterioration, or respiratory-rate *monitoring*, edges toward "monitoring physiological processes" (IIa), or toward vital parameters (IIb). Recommendation: drop cough tracking from the EU build, or restrict it to a private self-log with no interpretation.
- **AI Act, device route.** If the app stays a non-device, the Annex I high-risk route does not apply. One point needs legal review: an ML model that infers craving, an internal state, from physiological signals like HRV could arguably be analyzed under the AI Act's emotion-recognition and biometric definitions (Annex III and Art. 50 transparency). This is my inference and is not supported by any source found here.
- **GDPR operations.** Plan for: Art. 9(2)(a) explicit consent captured separately per data category (wearable, microphone, logs); a DPIA before launch (large-scale special-category processing); data minimization (process audio on device and discard raw audio); and an EU representative if the controller is not in the EU.

### Gaps

- I found no official EDPB text on wearables or DPIAs (one blog cites an "EDPB Opinion 04/2022", which I could not verify).
- The proposed "MDR 2.0" reform that could move devices within AI Act Annex I is mentioned only in passing and is UNVERIFIED.

## Q7. Apple App Store Review Guidelines (current version; 1.4.1, 1.4.2, 1.4.3, 5.1.1, 5.1.2, 5.1.3)

## Q9. Claims analysis: acceptable general-wellness phrasing vs. phrasing that implies a medical-device or disease purpose

### Takeaway
Across all markets the same rule decides the outcome: **intended purpose, shown by claims, UI and marketing**. Keep claims in FDA Category 1 territory (general health, habits, relaxation, motivation). Describe outputs as **estimates and illustrations**. Never name diseases, give clinical thresholds, use "measure/diagnose/predict/treat" language, or make efficacy claims without RCT support. A disclaimer alone does not cure a disease-oriented claim (FDA and MHRA commentary).

### Cited Findings (principles the table relies on)
- **FDA.** Category 1 claims, with no disease reference, cover topics such as relaxation and stress management, fitness and sleep. Physiologic estimates may be shown as wellness values and trends, but disease references, diagnostic thresholds, clinical-grade claims and condition-management alerts are excluded. — [Fish & Richardson](https://www.fr.com/insights/thought-leadership/articles/fdas-draft-wellness-guidance-potentially-expands-the-universe-of-unregulated-products/); [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/fdas-2026-guidance-on-general-wellness-devices-policy-for-low-risk-devices/); [Hogan Lovells](https://hlc.com/en/publications/fda-town-hall-sheds-new-light-on-updated-general-wellness-policy)
- **FDA, smokers.** Reminders and motivational guidance for smokers trying to quit fall under enforcement discretion. — [FDA examples page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-software-functions-including-mobile-medical-applications/examples-software-functions-which-fda-will-exercise-enforcement-discretion)
- **EU.** "Prediction, prognosis" and "monitoring" of disease are medical purposes. Rule 11 makes monitoring physiological processes IIa (IIb for vital parameters). Well-being software is excluded. — [Johner](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/mdr-rule-11/); [NAMSA](https://namsa.com/resources/blog/mdr-stricter-rules-medical-device-software/)
- **Apple.** Methodology must be disclosed for accuracy claims, sensor-only vital-sign measurement is banned, and dosage calculators are restricted. — [Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) [fetched]
- **FTC.** Health claims need competent and reliable scientific evidence, and "may help" does not cure an unsupported claim. — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/ftc-issues-significant-updated-guidance-for-health-related-claims/)
- **Tobacco-dependence treatment is device territory.** — [Pharmazeutische Zeitung (NichtraucherHelden)](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/mit-dem-rauchen-aufhoeren-mit-digitaler-unterstuetzung-137255/); [MedCity (Pivot)](https://medcitynews.com/2021/07/carrot-gets-fda-nod-for-over-the-counter-use-of-smoking-cessation-device/)

Cross-cutting rules for UI and marketing (my synthesis):
- Use "estimate," "illustration," "trend," "insight," "support," "habit" and "relax." Avoid "measure," "diagnose," "detect," "predict" (disease), "treat," "cure," "clinical," "medical-grade," "abnormal" and "risk of [disease]."
- Never show traffic-light "normal/abnormal" bands on Lung Age, Bio-Toxicity, HRV or RHR. Prefer the user's own baseline and trend.
- Do not cite quit-rate statistics for the app itself unless an app-specific RCT supports them. Population facts (e.g., health benefits of quitting from WHO or CDC) can be cited as general education, attributed and dated.
- Keep one consistent claim set across the App Store, Play, the website, ads and social. FTC and MHRA look at all marketing.

### Gaps
- None of the regulators has published a worked example on "lung age" or smoking-recovery visualizations. The table applies general principles and should be checked by regulatory counsel in each market.
