## Q1. Biomarkers of exposure: exclusive vapers vs smokers vs dual users

### Takeaway
People who vape and do not smoke have much lower exposure than smokers to tobacco-specific nitrosamines (TSNAs), PAHs and most VOCs (about 10–98% lower, depending on the biomarker), while their nicotine intake is similar. Dual users are exposed to toxicants **at least as much as exclusive smokers, and sometimes more**, and their exposure rises with cigarettes per day. The exposure model should therefore give **no "vaping discount"** to a dual user's cigarette count.

### Cited Findings
- **Shahab L, Goniewicz ML, Blount BC, et al. (2017)** "Nicotine, Carcinogen, and Toxin Exposure in Long-Term E-Cigarette and Nicotine Replacement Therapy Users: A Cross-sectional Study." *Ann Intern Med* 166(6):390–400. DOI 10.7326/M16-1107. UK, n=181 across 5 purposively recruited groups of about 36–37 each: cigarette-only; long-term (≥6 months) ex-smokers using only e-cigarettes or only NRT; long-term dual cigarette+EC users and dual cigarette+NRT users. — [PubMed 28166548](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28166548/); [Stirling repository](https://dspace.stir.ac.uk/handle/1893/24693?mode=full)
- In Shahab 2017, salivary and urinary nicotine biomarkers showed "no clear between-group differences" once confounders were controlled for. E-cigarette-only and NRT-only users had "significantly lower metabolite levels for TSNAs (including … NNAL) and VOCs" than cigarette-only smokers. — [PubMed 28166548](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28166548/); [Pharmaceutical Journal news](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/smokers-who-swap-cigarettes-for-e-cigarettes-or-nrt-have-lower-carcinogen-levels)
- Shahab, in an interview: "95 percent reduction in some biomarkers for e-cigarette users versus smokers". This is a press quote, not an abstract figure. — [The Analytical Scientist](https://www.theanalyticalscientist.com/issues/2018/articles/mar/the-human-element/)
- **Goniewicz ML, Smith DM, Edwards KC, et al. (2018)** "Comparison of Nicotine and Toxicant Exposure in Users of Electronic Cigarettes and Combustible Cigarettes." *JAMA Netw Open* 1(8):e185937 (DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.5937, DOI recalled). PATH Study Wave 1 biomarker subsample, n=5,105. — [UB ResearchConnect](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/comparison-of-nicotine-and-toxicant-exposure-in-users-of-electron/); [EPA HERO](https://hero.epa.gov/hero/index.cfm/reference/details/reference_id/5114304)
- Goniewicz 2018: exclusive e-cigarette users had **10% to 98% significantly lower** concentrations of exposure biomarkers (TSNAs, PAHs, most VOCs, nicotine) than exclusive cigarette smokers. They still had **higher** nicotine, TSNA, VOC and metal biomarkers than never-users. — [Roswell Park release](https://www.roswellpark.org/media/news/e-cigarette-users-have-lower-exposure-toxicants-long-they-dont-smoke-combustible); [UB ResearchConnect](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/comparison-of-nicotine-and-toxicant-exposure-in-users-of-electron/)
- Goniewicz 2018: "exclusive cigarette users showed 10% to 36% lower concentrations of several biomarkers … than dual users". Toxicant exposure was **greatest among dual users**, and among dual users the frequency of combustible cigarette use correlated positively with toxicant concentration. — [UB ResearchConnect](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/comparison-of-nicotine-and-toxicant-exposure-in-users-of-electron/)
- Two related studies point the same way. In Smith et al. (PATH, *Nicotine Tob Res*), patterns of e-cigarette use had little effect on dual users' toxicant exposure. In a Canadian crossover (Czoli et al.), biomarkers were significantly lower during exclusive vaping than during dual use. — [NAQC summary of Czoli et al.](https://www.naquitline.org/news/418856/Biomarkers-of-Exposure-Among-Dual-Users-of-Tobacco-Cigarettes-and-Electronic-Cigarettes-in-Canada..htm); [CDC Stacks](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/105958)
- **NASEM (2018)** *Public Health Consequences of E-Cigarettes*. National Academies Press (DOI 10.17226/24952, DOI recalled). It found "**conclusive evidence** that completely substituting e-cigarettes for combustible tobacco cigarettes reduces users' exposure to numerous toxicants and carcinogens", and "**substantial evidence**" that nicotine exposure in experienced adult vapers "can be comparable to that from combustible tobacco cigarettes". — [NASEM ch. 3](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24952/chapter/3); [Medical News Today summary](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/320733); [WA DOH summary](https://doh.wa.gov/Portals/1/Documents/Pubs/340-337-VapingHealthImpacts.pdf)
- NASEM evidence tiers: "conclusive" means multiple high-quality studies; "substantial" means a firm conclusion that cannot fully exclude chance or bias. NASEM also reported that complete switching reduces adverse short-term health outcomes, and that oxidative stress from e-cigarettes is generally lower than from combustible tobacco, with long-term consequences unknown. — [WA DOH summary](https://doh.wa.gov/Portals/1/Documents/Pubs/340-337-VapingHealthImpacts.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Derived:** If exclusive smokers are 10–36% lower than dual users on several biomarkers, dual users are about **1.11–1.56×** an exclusive smoker on those biomarkers (1/0.90 to 1/0.64). Two explanations fit this: dual users in PATH may smoke more, and vaping adds exposure. Cross-sectional data cannot tell them apart.
- **Model rule:** For a dual user, the combustion and carcinogen tiers should use the **cigarettes they actually smoke**, weighted 1.0 with no reduction for vaping. Add the vaping terms on top. Do not offset cigarettes with vaping.
- Nicotine tier: a long-term exclusive vaper's nicotine intake can match smoking (Shahab; NASEM). The nicotine tier therefore should **not** fall automatically when someone switches.
- Carcinogen tier for exclusive vaping: the lower bound "98% lower" applies to TSNAs and PAHs, while some VOCs and metals are reduced far less (the "10%" end). A single carcinogen weight hides this. One carcinogen weight of about 0.02–0.3 relative to a cigarette (central about 0.05) is defensible; per-class weights would be more accurate.

### Gaps
- Biomarker-specific % differences (NNAL, 3-HPMA for acrolein, CEMA, CYMA, 1-HOP, metals) could not be extracted because full texts were blocked. **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** Shahab 2017 reported NNAL about **97% lower** in long-term e-cigarette-only users than in cigarette-only smokers, and dual users' levels did not differ meaningfully from smokers'. Verify both in the full text.
- **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** Shahab 2017 was funded by Cancer Research UK. Some co-authors have historically received funding from or consulted for NRT manufacturers. Check the disclosure section.
- **OHID/McNeill 2022** "Nicotine vaping in England: 2022 evidence update" (biomarker systematic review) was **not retrieved**: the search budget ran out. **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** It concluded that vaping exposes users to a much lower level of toxicants than smoking, and that dual users' biomarker levels are generally similar to, or only modestly below, smokers' unless cigarette consumption falls substantially. Verify against the report on gov.uk.
- I found no meta-analysis (2022–2026) of biomarker ratios for dual users vs exclusive smokers that adjusts for CPD.
- No CO/COHb figures for exclusive vapers were retrieved this session. Physiologically, e-cigarettes involve no combustion, so CO comes only from environmental sources. This is an inference, not a cited figure.

## Q2. Respiratory and cardiovascular outcomes of vaping and dual use (incl. EVALI)

### Takeaway
Exclusive vaping is associated with **more respiratory symptoms than not using any product, but fewer than smoking**. Dual use has risk at least equal to smoking and roughly additive. Some exclusive-vaper associations weaken after adjusting for prior smoking or among never-smokers, so evidence of an independent lung-structure effect is weak and contested. On cardiovascular outcomes, a randomized trial shows endothelial function improves within 1 month of switching to e-cigarettes, whether or not the e-liquid contains nicotine.

### Cited Findings
- **Bhatta DN, Glantz SA (2020)** "Association of E-Cigarette Use With Respiratory Disease Among Adults: A Longitudinal Analysis." *Am J Prev Med* 58(2):182–190; epub 16 Dec 2019. DOI 10.1016/j.amepre.2019.07.028. PATH Waves 1–3 (Sept 2013 to Oct 2016). Respiratory disease was self-reported COPD, chronic bronchitis, emphysema or asthma. The authors report e-cigarette use was associated with respiratory disease **independent of combustible use**. Dual use, the most common pattern, carried risk beyond either product alone. — [UCSF Tobacco Center](https://tobacco.ucsf.edu/node/81411); [EHIDC PDF copy](https://www.ehidc.org/sites/default/files/resources/files/Association%20of%20e%20cigarette%20use%20with%20respiratory%20disease%20among%20adults.pdf); [MDedge/Hospitalist](https://mdedge.com/content/dual-e-cigarette-and-combustible-tobacco-use-compound-respiratory-disease-risk)
- Limitations the authors acknowledged: possible recall bias, no distinction between daily and non-daily use, and conditions combined to gain power. — [MDedge/Hospitalist](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/dual-e-cigarette-and-combustible-tobacco-use-compound-respiratory-disease-risk)
- **Contested:** Kenkel et al. (NBER Working Paper w27507; VoxEU column) could closely replicate Bhatta & Glantz, but "among respondents who had never smoked combustible tobacco, we find no evidence that current or former e-cigarette use is associated with respiratory disease". They argue the original model wrongly assumed the same association for never, former and current smokers. — [NBER w27507](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27507.pdf); [CEPR/VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/research-claims-link-between-e-cigarettes-and-respiratory-disease-not-so-fast)
- **MGH longitudinal PATH analysis** (Reddy, Rigotti et al.; journal per recall below). One-year respiratory symptom incidence was **10.7%** with neither product, **11.8%** exclusive vaping, **17.1%** exclusive smoking and **19.7%** dual use. Dual users had **1.9×** the odds of new symptoms compared with exclusive vapers. Exclusive vapers were **not significantly** different from non-users. — [Respiratory Therapy](https://respiratory-therapy.com/public-health/smoking/vaping-ecigarettes/vaping-smoking-tobacco-worse-outcomes/)
- **PATH Wave 2 cross-sectional** (Li et al., *Tobacco Control*, 2019, per secondary summary), n=28,171 adults. Exclusive vapers vs non-users: wheeze and related symptoms **aOR 1.67**. Exclusive vapers vs exclusive smokers: **aOR 0.68**. Dual users vs current smokers: no significant difference. — [NAQC newsroom (secondary)](https://www.naquitline.org/news/news.asp?id=442970) (primary paper not directly retrieved)
- **PATH cross-sectional (2022, PubMed 35366322):** "Past and current cigarette smoking drove functionally important respiratory symptoms, while exclusive use of other tobacco products was largely not associated". The e-cigarette association "was sensitive to adjustment for pack-years". — [PubMed 35366322](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35366322/)
- **PATH longitudinal transitions (2024)** *Nicotine Tob Res*, DOI 10.1093/ntr/ntae224, "Association of Cigarette/E-cigarette Transitions with Respiratory Symptom Resolution". Switching **completely** from cigarettes to e-cigarettes was associated with **higher** rates of wheeze resolution. Moving to dual use **without reducing smoking intensity** was associated with **lower** resolution rates than continued smoking. — [CiteDrive record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/association-of-cigarettee-cigarette-transitions-with-respiratory-symptom-resolution/); [ScienceBlog](https://scienceblog.com/549379/switching-completely-to-e-cigarettes-improves-smokers-breathing-study-finds/)
- A 2023 systematic review found respiratory symptoms common across exclusive vapers, dual users and switchers, but the evidence was too heterogeneous to establish causation. — [PMC10720266](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10720266/) (secondary description via search)
- **George J, Hussain M, Vadiveloo T, et al. (2019)** "Cardiovascular Effects of Switching From Tobacco Cigarettes to Electronic Cigarettes" (VESUVIUS). *J Am Coll Cardiol* 74(25):3112–3120. DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2019.09.067. Randomized trial with a parallel preference cohort; 114 UK smokers (≥15 cigarettes/day for ≥2 years). Arms: continue smoking (n=40), nicotine EC (n=37), nicotine-free EC (n=37). Switchers' FMD improved by **1.49 percentage points** vs continuing smokers at **1 month**. Nicotine content made **no apparent difference** to the benefit. Funder: British Heart Foundation. Caveats: small, short, single centre. — [qxmd/DOI](https://read.qxmd.com/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2019.09.067); [BHF](https://www.bhf.org.uk/what-we-do/our-research/impact-of-clinical-trials/vesuvius-trial); [HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/switching-from-tobacco-to-ecigarettes-could-improve-vascular-health)
- VESUVIUS authors note that in meta-analyses a sustained **1%** FMD improvement is associated with lower cardiovascular event risk. — [HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/switching-from-tobacco-to-ecigarettes-could-improve-vascular-health)

### Inferences
- **Derived "fraction of smoking excess" for exclusive vaping (respiratory symptoms):**
  - From Reddy/MGH: (11.8−10.7)/(17.1−10.7) = **0.17**.
  - From Li 2019: smokers vs non-users ≈ 1.67/0.68 ≈ 2.46 (approximate, because the two adjusted models differ). The log-odds fraction for vaping is ln1.67/ln2.46 ≈ **0.57**.
  - From never-smoker analyses (Kenkel): about **0**.
  - Suggested respiratory **symptom/irritation** weight for exclusive daily vaping: central **0.15–0.2**, range **0–0.6**, relative to the symptom excess of a current smoker. **Low confidence.**
- Dual use: Reddy shows dual-use excess (19.7−10.7 = 9.0 pp) at about **1.4×** the smoking excess (6.4 pp). Li shows no significant difference from smoking. A **≥1.0× smoking, roughly additive** treatment is supported (cigarette term + vaping irritation term).
- No source shows a **structural** lung-damage pathway for vaping that is comparable to pack-year accrual (for example emphysema or FEV1 decline). The pack-year (structural) tier for exclusive vaping should be about 0, with at most a small placeholder (≤0.05) for model humility. Symptoms and inflammation should go through a separate "airway irritation" state.
- Cardiovascular: endothelial dysfunction in smokers appears driven largely by combustion constituents, since nicotine-free and nicotine EC gave similar FMD gains. The endothelial/FMD component of the recovery curve can progress after switching to EC. Acute nicotine effects on heart rate and blood pressure are mechanistically separate and should stay tied to the nicotine tier (see Gaps on NASEM HR).

### Gaps
- **Bhatta & Glantz effect sizes not verified.** **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** current e-cigarette use aOR ≈ **1.29 (95% CI 1.03–1.61)**; former e-cigarette use ≈ **1.31**; current combustible use ≈ **2.56 (1.92–3.41)**; dual use ≈ **3.30** (multiplicative). Verify in the AJPM tables.
- **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** A separate Bhatta & Glantz paper (*J Am Heart Assoc* 2019) on e-cigarettes and myocardial infarction was **retracted in 2020**. The PATH analysis had not accounted for whether MIs occurred before e-cigarette initiation. This is a reliability flag for this research line, not for the AJPM paper itself. Verify.
- **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** Reddy KP, Schwamm E, Kalkhoran S, Noubary F, Walensky RP, Rigotti NA. "Respiratory Symptom Incidence among People Using Electronic Cigarettes, Combustible Tobacco, or Both." *Am J Respir Crit Care Med* 2021;204(2):231–234. Journal and citation not confirmed this session.
- **Osei et al. 2020** (*Am J Prev Med*; BRFSS 2016–17, e-cigarette use and COPD by smoking status) was not retrieved: search budget exhausted. No numbers available.
- **NASEM 2018 on heart rate:** **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** NASEM found "substantial evidence that e-cigarette use results in an increase in heart rate shortly after nicotine intake" and moderate evidence of increased blood pressure. The search did not confirm the wording.
- **EVALI** numbers were not retrieved. **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** CDC's final national count (18 Feb 2020) was **2,807** hospitalized cases and **68** deaths. Vitamin E acetate, mostly in THC-containing products from informal sources, was strongly linked. EVALI should be a **product-type flag** (illicit or THC cartridges), not part of nicotine-vaping exposure weights. Verify at cdc.gov.
- No 2022–2026 RCT or cohort with spirometry for exclusive vapers vs smokers vs never users was retrieved. Lung-function trajectories for switchers remain an evidence gap here.

## Q3. Heated tobacco products (HTPs, e.g., IQOS)

### Takeaway
HTP emissions and short-term exposure biomarkers are substantially lower than for cigarettes: ≥62% lower for harmful and potentially harmful constituents (HPHCs), ≥75% lower for particulate matter, and 42–96% lower human exposure in switching studies. However, the evidence is **dominated by industry-funded studies**. Exposure stays **above abstinence** (NNAL higher in HTP users than in quitters). Regulators allowed an exposure claim and **refused a risk claim**.

### Cited Findings
- **Simonavicius E, McNeill A, Shahab L, Brose LS (2019)** "Heat-not-burn tobacco products: a systematic literature review." *Tob Control* 28(5):582–594; online Sept 2018. DOI 10.1136/tobaccocontrol-2018-054419. Of 31 studies, **20 were tobacco-industry affiliated**. Results were "broadly consistent between independent and industry-funded studies". — [PubMed 30181382](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30181382/); [PMC6824610](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6824610/); [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10053670/)
- Simonavicius 2019: HPHC emissions were reduced by **≥62%** and particulate matter by **≥75%** vs cigarettes. In experimental human-use studies, all on a single product, toxicant exposure fell by **42% to 96%**. Users and bystanders are still exposed to toxicants, at substantially lower levels than with cigarettes. — [Tob Control abstract](https://tobaccocontrol.bmj.com/content/28/5/582.abstract); [PMC6824610](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6824610/)
- **Tattan-Birch H, Hartmann-Boyce J, Kock L, et al. (2022)** "Heated tobacco products for smoking cessation and reducing smoking prevalence." *Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 2022, Issue 1, CD013790. DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD013790.pub2. 13 completed studies, including 11 RCTs on safety (2,666 participants). **All RCTs were tobacco-industry funded.** 8 RCTs were at unclear risk of bias and 3 at high risk. — [Cochrane CD013790](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013790.pub2); [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10141686/); [PMC8733777](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8733777)
- Tattan-Birch 2022: moderate-certainty evidence that urinary **NNAL at follow-up was higher in HTP users than in the abstinence group** (log mean difference **0.50, 95% CI 0.34–0.66**; 5 studies, 382 participants). Evidence on adverse events vs abstinence was insufficient. — [Cochrane abstract PDF](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013790.pub2/pdf/CDSR/CD013790/CD013790_abstract.pdf)
- **US FDA, 7 July 2020:** exposure-modification orders under the modified-risk tobacco product (MRTP) pathway for PMI's IQOS (device plus Marlboro HeatSticks, Smooth Menthol and Fresh Menthol). Permitted claims: IQOS heats rather than burns tobacco, which significantly reduces the production of harmful chemicals; switching completely "significantly reduces your body's exposure to harmful or potentially harmful chemicals". PMI's **risk**-modification request was **denied**. Exposure modification is a lower standard. The orders require post-market surveillance, including youth uptake. An IQOS 3 order later reused the same exposure claim. — [NAQC on FDA](https://www.naquitline.org/news/517720/FDA-Completes-Scientific-Review-Authorizes-Reduced-Exposure-Marketing-for-IQOS-Heated-Tobacco-Syste.htm); [FDA document](https://fda.gov/media/156825/download); [CSP Daily News](https://www.cspdailynews.com/tobacco/fda-issues-iqos-3-modified-risk-granted-order)
- FDA's Tobacco Products Scientific Advisory Committee (TPSAC) recommended **against** authorizing IQOS as a reduced-risk product. PMI has used the FDA order in promotion (Tobacco Tactics; an advocacy source). — [Tobacco Tactics](https://tobaccotactics.org/?p=6989)

### Inferences
- **Derived HTP weights per heatstick, relative to one cigarette:**
  - Particulate/lung tier: ≤0.25 by PM emissions (≥75% lower) to 0.04–0.58 by human exposure (42–96% lower). Central about **0.2**, range **0.05–0.6**, confidence **moderate-low** (industry-dominated, single-product human data).
  - Carcinogen tier: same range, central **0.2**. NNAL in switchers stays above quitters. Derived: if the log mean difference is on the natural-log scale, exp(0.50) ≈ **1.65×** abstainers (95% CI about 1.40–1.93×). Treat it as 0.05–0.6 of a cigarette, never 0.
  - CO tier: **near 0**, low confidence. No CO-specific HTP number was retrieved this session. Treat it as an open item.
  - Nicotine tier: about **0.7–1.0**, **low confidence (not retrieved)**.
- Lower **exposure** does not establish lower **risk**. The FDA explicitly refused the risk claim. App copy should not say HTPs "heal" the lungs. The recovery curve could progress the CO and symptom components only partially (see Q8).

### Gaps
- **WHO 2020 statement on HTPs** was not retrieved. **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** WHO said that reduced exposure to harmful chemicals in HTPs does not make them harmless, nor does it translate into reduced risk to human health. Verify on who.int.
- **Independent HTP studies from 2022–2026** were not retrieved, apart from the existence of a 2025 systematic review: "Assessing the Health Impacts of Heated Tobacco Products Compared to Traditional Tobacco Use", [PMC12637409](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12637409/). Its contents were not read.
- HTP CO and COHb in users, nicotine delivery per stick vs cigarettes, and lung-inflammation biomarkers (FeNO, sputum): no values retrieved.
- The Cochrane HTP cessation estimate (HTP vs NRT or vs cigarettes) was not retrieved.

## Q4. Bidis (South Asia)

### Takeaway
A bidi holds about one-third of a cigarette's tobacco, yet bidi smokers show **higher breath CO** and **equal or higher all-cause mortality RR** than cigarette smokers in Indian cohorts. The only published pack-year convention found counts **1 bidi = 1 cigarette**. I found **no published source** for a 0.25 or 0.5 weighting.

### Cited Findings
- **Indian breath-CO study (2010)**, *Indian J Chest Dis Allied Sci* (PubMed 20364610), "Breath carbon monoxide concentration in cigarette and bidi smokers in India":
  - Mean tobacco weight was **216.8 mg per bidi vs 696 mg per cigarette**.
  - At **>5 pack-years**, breath CO was higher in bidi smokers (**18.9 ± 7.7 ppm**) than in cigarette smokers (**13.6 ± 5.8 ppm**).
  - The authors concluded that "one bidi may be considered to one cigarette for calculating 'pack-years'" and that bidi is "equally or more harmful than cigarette smoking".
  - [PubMed 20364610](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20364610/)
- **Gupta PC, Mehta HC (2000)** "Cohort study of all-cause mortality among tobacco users in Mumbai, India." *Bull World Health Organ* 78(7):877–883. Cohort of 52,568. Men: RR **1.39** for cigarette smokers and **1.78** for bidi smokers, with an apparent dose-response by smoking frequency. Women, mainly smokeless users: RR **1.35**. — [SciELO full text](https://scielosp.org/article/bwho/2000.v78n7/877-883); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/fbac8fca611a47bf9055f2eb68547af3)
- **Gupta PC, Pednekar MS, Parkin DM, Sankaranarayanan R (2005)**, Bombay Cohort Study (journal per recall below; 5,470 deaths). Adjusted RR **1.37 (95% CI 1.23–1.53)** for male cigarette smokers and **1.64 (1.47–1.81)** for male bidi smokers, each with dose-response. Female smokeless users: adjusted RR **1.25**. The authors concluded bidi is no less risky than cigarettes. — [GHDx](https://ghdx.healthdata.org/node/283149); [scholarcommons.sc.edu](https://scholarcommons.sc.edu/sph_epidemiology_biostatistics_facpub/117)
- **Jha P, Jacob B, Gajalakshmi V, et al. (2008)** "A nationally representative case-control study of smoking and death in India." *N Engl J Med* 358:1137–1147 (DOI 10.1056/NEJMsa0707719, DOI recalled).
  - Design: about 1.1 million homes. About 33,000 women and 41,000 men who had died were compared with living controls.
  - Smoking mortality RR at ages 30–69: **2.0 (99% CI 1.8–2.3) in women** and **1.7 (1.6–1.8) in men**.
  - **Tuberculosis** was the largest excess: RR **3.0** (women) and **2.3** (men).
  - Smoking accounted for about 1 in 5 male deaths and 1 in 20 female deaths at ages 30–69.
  - [St John's](https://www.stjohns.in/publications/a-nationally-representative-case-control-study-of-smoking-and-death-in-india); [Fogarty/NIH](https://fic.nih.gov/News/GlobalHealthMatters/Pages/Smoking-India.aspx); [India Environment Portal](https://www.indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/reports-and-documents/a-nationally-representative-case-control-study-of-smoking-and-death-in-india)
- Press coverage of Jha 2008 said that "even a few bidis a day raised mortality risk by about one-third", while the same number of cigarettes "nearly doubled" middle-age death risk. This is a secondary paraphrase; the exact bidi-specific RR was not retrieved. — [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/97238/)
- **PURE bidi substudy (2017)**, *Lancet Global Health* (Feb 2017), "Effects of bidi smoking on all-cause mortality and cardiorespiratory outcomes in men from south Asia" (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh). It did **not** convert bidis into cigarette pack-years: it analysed bidi and cigarette smokers separately, with >10 pack-years as the heavy-use threshold for either. Adjusted prevalence of chronic wheeze, cough/sputum, dyspnoea and chest pain rose across non-smokers < light smokers < heavy cigarette smokers < **heavy bidi smokers**. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/02bde0c07c914cb9ba6969287c61d0c8); [Manchester research portal](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-bidi-smoking-on-all-cause-mortality-and-cardiorespirat/)

### Inferences
- **Derived:**
  - Per-stick tobacco ratio: 216.8/696 ≈ **0.31**.
  - Breath-CO ratio per smoker: 18.9/13.6 ≈ **1.39**. This is per smoker, not per stick.
  - Excess-mortality ratio (RR−1), bidi vs cigarette: 0.64/0.37 ≈ **1.7** (Gupta 2005) and 0.78/0.39 ≈ **2.0** (Gupta & Mehta 2000). These are per smoker, not per stick, and bidi smokers may smoke more sticks per day.
- Bidis are hand-rolled in tendu leaf. People puff more often to keep them lit and inhale deeply, which helps explain why a stick with less tobacco can deliver at least as much smoke dose.
- **Recommendation:** count **1 bidi = 1.0 cigarette** (range **0.5–1.5**) in the combustion/CO and carcinogen tiers, with moderate confidence. A **0.25 weighting (by tobacco mass) would understate risk** given the CO and mortality data. The nicotine tier is uncertain: no per-stick nicotine yield was retrieved. Use central about 0.7 (range 0.3–1.2), low confidence.
- For the lung model in South Asian users, the large **TB risk excess** in smokers (RR 2.3–3.0) supports a region-aware TB-risk flag in the lung module (beyond Section 1.3).

### Gaps
- Machine-smoking yields of nicotine, tar and CO per bidi vs per cigarette were **not retrieved**. "3–5× CO/tar", from the brief, could not be confirmed this session. Candidate primary sources: Malson JL et al., *Tob Control* 2001 (bidi nicotine content) and *Pharmacol Biochem Behav* 2002 (bidi CO/nicotine delivery). Both are unverified.
- Lung-function (FEV1) data specific to bidi smokers were not retrieved.
- PURE-bidi mortality hazard ratios were not retrieved.
- I found no Indian COPD study using a fractional "bidi pack-year" weight of 0.25 or 0.5. If the blueprint team has seen one, cite it directly. Note that the PURE substudy kept bidi pack-years on the same scale as cigarette pack-years, which implies 1:1.

## Q5. Hookah / waterpipe

### Takeaway
Per session (about 45 min), waterpipe smoking produces about **9×** the expired-CO boost and about **3×** the peak COHb of one cigarette. Smoke volume is about 50–125× one cigarette and tar about 25×. Peak plasma nicotine is similar to one cigarette. Lung function in waterpipe smokers is lower than in non-smokers and **not significantly different from cigarette smokers**. Converting sessions into cigarette-equivalents is only possible with wide uncertainty, so the uncertainty should be stated explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- **Eissenberg T, Shihadeh A (2009)** "Waterpipe Tobacco and Cigarette Smoking: Direct Comparison of Toxicant Exposure." *Am J Prev Med* 37(6):518–523 (DOI 10.1016/j.amepre.2009.07.014, DOI recalled). Crossover in 31 participants: waterpipe for up to 45 min vs one cigarette.
  - Expired CO rose **23.9 ppm** (waterpipe) vs **2.7 ppm** (cigarette).
  - Peak COHb was **3.9%** vs **1.3%**.
  - Peak plasma nicotine was **10.2** vs **10.6 ng/mL**, not different.
  - Total puff volume was **48.6 L** vs **1.0 L**.
  - [PubMed 19944918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19944918/); [PMC2805076](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2805076); [VCU news](https://news.vcu.edu/article/waterpipe_tobacco_smokers_inhale_same_toxicants_as_cigarette)
- **Cobb, Shihadeh, Weaver, Eissenberg (2011)** replicated the pattern: peak COHb **4.5%** (waterpipe) vs **1.2%** (cigarette). — [WHO FCTC waterpipe database](https://extranet.who.int/fctcapps/fctcapps/fctc/kh/wts/wts-database/waterpipe-tobacco-and-cigarette-smoking-direct-comparison)
- **Primack BA, Carroll MV, Weiss PM, et al. (2016)** "Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Inhaled Toxicants from Waterpipe and Cigarette Smoking." *Public Health Rep* 131(1):76–85. DOI 10.1177/003335491613100114. **Note:** published in *Public Health Reports*, not *Nicotine Tob Res* as the brief assumed. 17 studies pooled; one waterpipe session compared with one cigarette.
  - Smoke volume about **74.1 L vs 0.6 L**.
  - Press release: about **125×** the smoke, **25×** the tar, **2.5×** the nicotine and **10×** the CO of one cigarette.
  - The authors caution that the comparison is imperfect: cigarette smokers may smoke about 20 per day, while hookah users may have only a few sessions.
  - [PMC4716475](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4716475); [UPMC release](https://www.upmc.com/media/news/primack-hookah-meta); [AUB ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.aub.edu.lb/items/89e3a876-4713-44a0-ae78-d8e99ba404e5)
- **Raad D, Gaddam S, Schunemann HJ, Irani J, Abou Jaoude P, Honeine R, Akl EA (2011)** "Effects of water-pipe smoking on lung function: a systematic review and meta-analysis." *Chest* 139(4):764–774 (DOI 10.1378/chest.10-0991, DOI recalled). Six cross-sectional studies.
  - Waterpipe smokers vs non-smokers: FEV1 **SMD −0.43 (95% CI −0.58 to −0.29)**, about **4.04%** lower FEV1 % predicted.
  - Waterpipe smokers vs **cigarette smokers**: **no statistically significant difference** in FEV1, FVC or FEV1/FVC.
  - The authors concluded waterpipe smoking "may be as harmful as cigarette smoking" and noted methodological limitations.
  - [UB ResearchConnect](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/effects-of-water-pipe-smoking-on-lung-function-a-systematic-revie/); [McMaster Experts](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/1520773)

### Inferences
- **Derived per-session ratios vs one cigarette:**
  - Expired-CO boost: 23.9/2.7 ≈ **8.9×**.
  - Peak COHb: 3.9/1.3 = **3.0×** (Cobb 2011: 4.5/1.2 ≈ 3.75×).
  - CO by meta-analysis: **10×**.
  - Tar: **25×**.
  - Smoke volume: about **49×** (Eissenberg) and about **124×** (Primack).
  - Nicotine: **≈1×** (peak plasma) to **2.5×** (yield).
- **Recommended per-session weights (central [range]):**
  - **Acute CO / bio-toxicity tier:** use the measured session boost directly in the acute model (eCO +24 ppm, COHb peak about 4–4.5%), not cigarette-equivalents.
  - **Lung-structural tier:** central **≈5 cigarette-equivalents per session**, range **1–25**, **very low confidence**. The central value is the geometric mean of the COHb ratio (3) and eCO ratio (9). The upper bound is the tar ratio. The lower bound is the nicotine-matched plasma level. The Raad finding (FEV1 similar to cigarette smokers) does not contradict a central value of about 5 for someone with several sessions per week.
  - **Nicotine tier:** central **≈1.6** (range 1–2.5), the geometric mean of 1 and 2.5.
  - **Carcinogen tier:** central **≈5** (range 1–25), very low confidence. Charcoal adds CO and PAHs, and much of the "tar" mass is glycerol/humectant. Tar mass is therefore a poor carcinogen proxy.
- Practical input: ask for **sessions/week**, typical session length and whether the pipe is **shared**. The 45-min crossover reflects a single smoker. Converting to pack-year-equivalents is lung-tier weight × sessions/day ÷ 20 × years.

### Gaps
- **Akl EA et al. (2010)** "The effects of waterpipe tobacco smoking on health outcomes: a systematic review." *Int J Epidemiol* 39(3):834–857 was not retrieved. **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** Pooled associations: lung cancer OR about 2.12, respiratory illness OR about 2.3, low birth weight OR about 2.12, periodontal disease OR about 3.0. Verify. **[UNVERIFIED RECALL]** An updated meta-analysis, Waziry R et al., *Int J Epidemiol* 2017, also exists.
- No published, validated "waterpipe-years" to pack-years conversion was retrieved.
- The effects of shared sessions and of herbal "tobacco-free" shisha (which still produces CO from charcoal) were not retrieved.

## Q8. Recovery-curve adjustments when a user quits cigarettes but keeps vaping (or uses HTP/smokeless)

### Takeaway
The evidence supports splitting recovery into components.
- **Progress after switching to e-cigarettes:** CO clearance (no combustion), endothelial function (FMD +1.49 pp within 1 month in VESUVIUS, nicotine or not), and respiratory symptom resolution (better with complete switching).
- **Partly impaired:** mucociliary clearance (nicotine-containing vapor impairs MCC via TRPA1; VG aerosols reduce CFTR activity in humans), airway protease/inflammatory markers (neutrophil elastase and MMP-9 elevated in vapers about as much as in smokers, in small bronchoscopy studies), and acute nicotine haemodynamics.

### Cited Findings
- **Chung S, Baumlin N, Dennis JS, et al. (2019)** "Electronic Cigarette Vapor with Nicotine Causes Airway Mucociliary Dysfunction Preferentially via TRPA1 Receptors." *Am J Respir Crit Care Med* 200(9):1134–1145. DOI 10.1164/rccm.201811-2087OC.
  - Nicotine-containing EC vapor reduced mucociliary function, including **in vivo sheep tracheal mucus velocity**.
  - The effect was mediated mainly by **TRPA1**, not nicotinic receptors. It worked by increasing mucus viscosity and reducing airway surface liquid volume.
  - **Correction to the brief:** the 2019 paper's main finding concerns **nicotine via TRPA1**, not PG/VG.
  - [PMC6888648](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6888648); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/9jz940x1)
- **Same group, 2022**, *Front Pharmacol*, DOI 10.3389/fphar.2022.1012723. **VG-containing** aerosols (VG or PG/VG), but not PG alone, reduced **nasal CFTR activity** in human volunteers who vaped for 7 days. A 2023 study found **PG** aerosols impair BK-channel activity and mucociliary parameters. — [Frontiers full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2022.1012723/full); [PMC10042605](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10042605)
- **Ghosh A, Coakley RC, Mascenik T, et al. (2018)** "Chronic E-Cigarette Exposure Alters the Human Bronchial Epithelial Proteome." *Am J Respir Crit Care Med* 198(1):67–76.
  - Bronchoscopy samples from non-smokers (n=8), smokers (n=9) and vapers (n=9).
  - **191** proteins were altered in vapers vs **292** in smokers, with only **79** shared.
  - MUC5AC was raised in both groups, and vaper airways looked abnormal at bronchoscopy.
  - [Thoracic VIPPN summary](https://vippn.thoracic.org/editors-choice/index.php); [PMC5821909](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5821909)
- **Ghosh A et al. (2019)** "Chronic E-Cigarette Use Increases Neutrophil Elastase and Matrix Metalloprotease Levels in the Lung." *Am J Respir Crit Care Med* 200(11):1392–1401. Neutrophil elastase, MMP-2 and MMP-9 activity and protein were **equally elevated** in BAL from vapers and smokers relative to non-smokers. Antiprotease levels were unchanged. — [PMC6884043](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6884043); [UCSF Tobacco Center](https://tobacco.ucsf.edu/node/76236)
- VESUVIUS: FMD improved by **1.49 pp** vs continued smoking at **1 month** after switching to EC, with no nicotine dependence of the benefit. — [BHF](https://www.bhf.org.uk/what-we-do/our-research/impact-of-clinical-trials/vesuvius-trial)
- PATH transitions (2024): complete switching was associated with higher wheeze resolution; dual use without CPD reduction had lower resolution than continued smoking. — [ScienceBlog](https://scienceblog.com/549379/switching-completely-to-e-cigarettes-improves-smokers-breathing-study-finds/)
- MGH/PATH: exclusive vapers' one-year symptom incidence (11.8%) was not significantly different from non-users' (10.7%). — [Respiratory Therapy](https://respiratory-therapy.com/public-health/smoking/vaping-ecigarettes/vaping-smoking-tobacco-worse-outcomes/)
- HTP users' NNAL stays above that of abstainers (Cochrane 2022). — [Cochrane CD013790](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013790.pub2)

### Inferences
Recommended recovery-component multipliers (fraction of the full "quit everything" recovery rate). All are **design inferences** from the evidence above, not published constants.

## Q9. Cessation outcomes: does vaping or dual use help or hinder quitting?

### Takeaway
High-certainty RCT evidence (Cochrane 2025) shows nicotine e-cigarettes produce **more 6-month+ quits than NRT**: RR **1.55 (95% CI 1.28–1.88)**, about **+3 quitters per 100**. Observational PATH analyses (Bhatta & Glantz) report lower quit odds among e-cigarette users. These are confounded and contested. Dual use is best modelled as a **transitional state**: the app should push toward complete switching or quitting rather than penalise vaping per se.

### Cited Findings
- **Lindson N, Livingstone-Banks J, Butler AR, McRobbie H, Bullen CR, Hajek P, Wu AD, Begh R, Theodoulou A, Notley C, Rigotti NA, Turner T, Fanshawe T, Hartmann-Boyce J (2025).** "Electronic cigarettes for smoking cessation." *Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 2025, Issue 11, CD010216.pub10. DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub10. Searches to **1 March 2025**. — [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub10/full); [UEA ePrints](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/98363); [Oxford PHC](https://www.phc.ox.ac.uk/publications/publication_modal/2338315)

About **3 more quitters per 100 (95% CI 2–5)**. — [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub10/full); [nicquit.com.au summary](https://nicquit.com.au/cochrane-review-vapes-vs-nrt-smoking-cessation/)

The Pharmaceutical Journal reports 80 RCTs and 29,861 participants (9 new), dated 26 August 2026. — [Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/vapes-more-effective-than-other-smoking-cessation-methods-cochrane-review-finds); [Cochrane news 2026](https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/vapes-help-more-people-quit-smoking-other-methods); [Vaping Post (vaping-trade media)](https://www.vapingpost.com/2026/10/08/vaping-quit-smoking-cochrane-review/)

The authors recommended e-cigarettes **not** be recommended. — [MDedge/Hospitalist](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/dual-e-cigarette-and-combustible-tobacco-use-compound-respiratory-disease-risk)
- HTPs: all RCTs in the 2022 Cochrane HTP review were tobacco-industry funded. Its cessation estimate was not retrieved. — [Cochrane CD013790](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013790.pub2)

### Inferences
- For a user's predicted quit probability, the RCT evidence (EC as a cessation aid) outranks observational PATH associations, which are confounded by heavier, more dependent smokers taking up vaping. The app can present nicotine EC as an evidence-based quit aid where legal, while flagging **dual use without CPD reduction** as producing no lung-tier benefit (Q1, Q2, Q8).

## Q11. Recommended explicit weighting scheme (three tiers) with uncertainty

### Takeaway
Replace a single "cigarette-equivalent pack-year" with **three parallel exposure accumulators**:
- **(a) combustion/particulate + CO (lung tier):** drives the lung-damage model and acute CO toxicity.
- **(b) nicotine (CV and addiction tier):** drives acute HR/BP, endothelial and dependence.
- **(c) carcinogen tier**, split into lung vs oral sites.

Each product has its own weight per natural unit, with a range and a confidence grade. Dual use is **additive**, with no vaping discount on cigarettes smoked.

### Cited Findings
(The weights below are derived from findings cited in Q1–Q9. Key anchors:)
- E-cigarette users' toxicant biomarkers are 10–98% lower than smokers'; dual users are at least as high as exclusive smokers. — [UB ResearchConnect (Goniewicz 2018)](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/comparison-of-nicotine-and-toxicant-exposure-in-users-of-electron/)
- E-cigarette nicotine intake is comparable to smoking. — [PubMed 28166548 (Shahab 2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28166548/); [NASEM 2018](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24952/chapter/3)
- HTP: ≥62% HPHC and ≥75% PM reductions; 42–96% lower human exposure. — [PMC6824610](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6824610/)
- Bidi: 1 bidi = 1 cigarette pack-year convention; higher breath CO; mortality RR 1.64–1.78 vs 1.37–1.39 for cigarettes. — [PubMed 20364610](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20364610/); [SciELO](https://scielosp.org/article/bwho/2000.v78n7/877-883)
- Waterpipe session vs cigarette: eCO +23.9 vs +2.7 ppm; COHb 3.9% vs 1.3%; similar plasma nicotine; 25× tar, 10× CO, 2.5× nicotine (meta-analysis). — [PubMed 19944918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19944918/); [UPMC](https://www.upmc.com/media/news/primack-hookah-meta)

### Inferences

**Model form (proposal):**

```
For each day d and product p (units_p,d in natural units):
  E_lung(d)  = CPD_cig(d) * 1.0 + Σ_p wL_p * units_p,d          # combustion / particulate
  E_CO(d)    = measured or modelled per-event CO boost (not cig-eq) # acute tox model
  E_nic(d)   = CPD_cig(d) * 1.0 + Σ_p wN_p * units_p,d          # CV / addiction
  E_carc_lung(d), E_carc_oral(d) = Σ_p wC_p,site * units_p,d
  E_irrit(d) = 1 if any inhaled non-combusted aerosol use that day (EC/HTP) else 0   # airway-irritation state
Pack-year_eq (lung) = Σ_days E_lung(d) / 20 / 365.25
Dual use: no subtraction; tiers are additive. Optionally apply a dual-use multiplier on E_lung of 1.0 (range 1.0–1.5)
          to reflect Goniewicz 2018 dual > exclusive smoker biomarkers (derived 1.11–1.56× on several biomarkers).
```

**Implementation notes**
1. **Units and inputs.** Bidi, kretek and cigarillo: sticks/day. Hookah: sessions/week, minutes per session, shared or not. HTP: sticks/day. EC: days/week plus a self-rated "about how many cigarettes' worth"; ml/day × mg/ml is optional. Smokeless: uses/day and product type. Pouches: pouches/day and mg strength.
2. **Display uncertainty.** Carry the central value plus low/high bounds through the lung model. Show the avatar's damage as a band when non-cigarette products make up more than about 20% of E_lung (a design choice).
3. **Regional defaults.**
   - South Asia: bidi, gutka/khaini/mishri common; turn on the TB-risk flag (Jha 2008 TB RR 2.3–3.0).
   - Middle East / North Africa: hookah sessions.
   - Southeast Asia (Indonesia): kretek.
   - Northern Europe: snus and pouches.
4. **Never subtract.** Vaping or HTP use never reduces E_lung from cigarettes actually smoked. Lung-tier credit comes only from fewer combusted units.
5. **Update cadence.** The Cochrane EC review is living (2025 RR 1.55; a possible 2026 update reports RR 1.61, unverified). Re-pull estimates at each update.

### Gaps
- The nicotine-tier weights for HTP, snus, gutka/khaini and pouches lack retrieved per-unit nicotine data. Large-cigar, cigarillo and kretek weights are placeholders. The hookah lung and carcinogen weights span a 25-fold range. All of these need verification against primary sources the session could not reach: NCBI/Cochrane/BMJ fetches were blocked, and the search budget ran out.
- No published, validated multi-product "cigarette-equivalent pack-year" algorithm was found. The scheme above is an original synthesis and should be presented as such, ideally with a sensitivity analysis.
